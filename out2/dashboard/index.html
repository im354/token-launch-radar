How this radar works

1The value model

Each protocol's implied value starts from its own TVL, never from a list of names. The board takes every protocol that already trades and has a TVL, divides its market cap by that TVL, and keeps the median multiple per category. A category needs at least three trading peers, otherwise the global median across all trading protocols stands in.

base = tvl_usd x category_multiple
growth = 1 + clamp(change_7d, -30, 60) / 100 x 0.5
maturity = 0.7 + 0.3 x min(years_listed, 5) / 5
implied_value_usd = round(base x growth x maturity)

The low and high ends of each row's band come from the 25th and 75th percentile multiples of the same category, so the band shows how much the answer moves between a cheap and an expensive peer set. Two adjustments bend the line. Growth: a protocol adding TVL is bid as if the trend persists, at half weight, capped at plus 60 percent and floored at minus 30 percent. Maturity: a protocol that has run longer without a token has had longer to accumulate supply to distribute, capped at five years so the effect cannot run away. A missing seven-day change counts as no change; a missing listing date counts as one year.

2What stays off the ranked board

A protocol is pre-token when it records no CoinGecko id and no symbol. Everything else already trades and is listed in the reference table above with its real market cap, never mixed into the ranking.

Some pre-token protocols are unlikely to issue a token of their own even though nothing trades yet: exchange-run products, bridges and canonical bridges, liquid staking and restaking categories, names carrying the whole words wrapped, staked or staking, names ending in a version marker, and names that begin with an exchange's name. Those are the same rules the watchlist uses. Nothing is deleted: each one stays on the board, is tagged unlikely, and states its reason on the row and in the detail view. It ranks below every plausible launch.

The watchlist is a floor, never a ceiling: every watchlist protocol is on the board, and so is every other pre-token protocol the snapshot holds.

3The airdrop assumption

Expected airdrop is a stated assumption, not a promise and not a forecast: implied value x 5 percent for a community allocation x 10 percent for the share an active farmer might receive. Both numbers are placeholders a reader should argue with. Nothing here says a protocol will issue a token, or when, or that any allocation exists. A farm plan describes work a person can do; it is not advice, and every step can lose money.

expected_airdrop_usd = implied_value_usd x 0.05 x 0.10
